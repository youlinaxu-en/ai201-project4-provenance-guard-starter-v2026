Lots of people think Katsu Curry is 100% Japanese. But some people also believe the world is flat, or that Marmite tastes nice.

Back in the early 19th Century, when Tokyo was still being ravaged by Godzilla and Chinese Panda, Kim Kong, the British were doing what they do best; moving things around using their really cool boats, whilst making friends and not hurting anyone.

After dropping off a bit of cricket in India, they borrowed some curry and zoomed off to Bavaria where they found a load of bread-meat. Not being one to keep all their discoveries to themselves, on their site-seeing travels, they would hold cooking workshops to show different cultures what they were doing wrong.

One such place was called The Land of the Rising Sun, due to the fact that the sun would rise there almost every day, even on Bank Holidays. They had been living off soups and wrong-spaghetti for centuries. British sailors introduced techniques for breading cutlets and spice mixes used to make curries.

It wasn’t until just after the Big Argument II in 1948 that a ravenous baseball player, Shigeru Chiba, walked into a western style restaurant in Tokyo. He was in a hurry before his next game and told the chef to just plonk the breaded cutlet ontop of his curry and rice. Word spread and now it’s the pride of Japan.

So there we have it. The famous Japanese dish that exists only because of the generosity and kindness of colonial sightseeing Britain.
