Cities around the world are increasingly turning to green infrastructure to address the challenges of climate change. Features such as urban parks, green roofs, and tree-lined streets help reduce temperatures, absorb rainwater, and improve air quality. These solutions also provide important benefits for residents, including opportunities for recreation and improved mental well-being. In addition, green spaces support local biodiversity by creating habitats for birds, insects, and other wildlife. Public transportation and cycling infrastructure further reduce emissions by giving people alternatives to private cars. While these initiatives require significant investment and careful planning, the long-term benefits often outweigh the initial costs. It is also essential for city leaders to involve communities in the decision-making process to ensure that improvements are shared fairly. Ultimately, sustainable urban design is a key strategy for building resilient cities that can thrive in a changing climate for future generations.
